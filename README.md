# Analysis of National Substance Use and Mental Health Services Data

Substance abuse in the U.S. not only harms individuals who misuse substances but also contributes to the decline of our healthcare, education, and social systems. Over the years, both substance abuse and mental health disorders have increasingly become more and more prevelant. As a result, the demand for national support services and resources has grown.

For this Exploratory Data Analysis and Visualization project, we will analyze data collected by SAMHSA on substance use and health services offered by treatment facilities across the United States from 2020 to 2024. The N-SUMHSS is a voluntary survey designed to collect data on the location, characteristics, and utilization of organized substance use and mental health treatment services for facilities. Three data collection modes were used; a secure web-based questionnaire, a paper questionnaire sent by mail, and a computer-assisted telephone interview.

The goal of our project is to investigate mental health and substance abuse in the U.S. by analyzing the distribution of specialized services

## Research Questions:
1. Are behavioral health facilities evenly distributed across the U.S.?
2. What types of services are most and least commonly offered?
3. How does facility focus affect services provided?
4. How does access to behavioral health services vary across
demographic groups​?
5. How do payment options vary across different service types?


## Findings
The findings reveal the distribution of behavioral health facilities varies across the U.S. with higher facility concentration in states with higher population sizes. While the more densely populated states such as California, Texas, Florida, and New York have a higher number of facilites, access does not scale proportionally with population. Some densely populated areas are underserved, while other less populated regions have limited facilites and services available. These geographical disparities highlight the gaps in access that can negatively impact individuals seeking care.

When looking at the specialized services provided by each facility, substance use treatment is the most commonly available service, often followed by short term recovery support such as discharge planning, housing assistance, and peer support services. However, mental health services are less accessible, despite the strong correlation between mental health conditions and substance use disorders. While many facilities offer immidiate treatment, fewer provide long-term support and checkups, which is important for sustainable recovery. The lack of provided ongoing care increases the risk of relapse and reduces individuals' chances for long term success.

The analysis reveals a clear relationship between primary treatment focus and services provided. Substance use facilites are more likely to offer recovery focused services, while mental health facilities tend to focus more on the treatment and less on post patient care. Access to care also varies significantly across demographic groups. Vulnerable populations such as LGBTQ+ individuals, adolescents, veterans, and active-duty military personal are less commonly served by specialized facilites. This uneven distribution highlights the disparities in access to behavioral health services, suggesting that certain populations face a more limited accessibility in specialized care.

Financial accessibility ia another key issue in this data. Many services provided by mental health and substance use facilities accept Medicaid, health insurance, and out-of-pocket payments. On the other hand, very few facilities can offer free treatment which further prevents individuals from accessing necessary care. This may lead to worsening mental health conditions, increased substance use, and broader challenges such as social stigmas and homelessness.

This project is important to society because it highlights the inequalities in mental health and substance use facilities, identifying areas where improvements are needed. Policymakers and public health organizations can use these findings to better allocate resources, expand facilities to underserved regions, and improve support for minorities and vulnerable populations. Businesses and healthcare sectors can use this analysis to inform decisions about where new facilities are needed, what services to prioritize, and how to create a more inclusive and accessible care facility.

There are many limitations in the dataset such as potential reporting bias since all data was collected through self-reported anonymous surveys. There are also several missing variables and values in the data which may affect the accuracy of the analysis. Finally, this dataset only focuses on facilities and provided services rather than patient outcomes, so we cannot analyze patient outcomes, quality of care, or actual patient experiences to facilities.
